Current research

Persona vectors for implicit elicitation

Early results

Can activation-level monitoring catch a model's traits when they're summoned indirectly? Early results say mostly yes, with a revealing exception for "evil." Built on Anthropic's safety-research persona-vectors work.

  • Interpretability

The question

Persona vectors are directions in a model's activation space that correspond to character traits such as evil, sycophancy, or hallucination. They can be extracted by contrasting prompts that do and don't elicit a trait, and then used to monitor a trait at inference time or steer it during training.

Chen et al. (2025) validate persona-vector monitoring only on explicit system prompts like "You are an evil assistant." But in deployment, traits are usually induced implicitly, through roleplay and situational framing rather than a named instruction (e.g. "You are playing a villain in a dark fantasy novel who believes the ends always justify the means"). My question:

Do persona vectors learned from explicit elicitation still detect a trait when it is elicited implicitly?

Hypothesis

I expected detection to transfer when a trait is genuinely present, but to vary by elicitation route, and predicted that "evil" in particular might need to be stated as a disposition rather than implied by situation alone.

Approach & setup

  • Model: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (8 queries across 6 layers per condition).
  • Traits: evil, sycophancy, and hallucination.
  • Elicitation conditions: Explicit (direct instruction), Described (a behavioral disposition without naming the trait), and Contextual (situational framing where the trait must be inferred).
  • Extraction & evaluation: trait vectors from contrasting explicit prompts, evaluated with an 8-level intensity spectrum per trait, 20 questions, and a shared judge rubric.
  • Metric: Pearson correlation (r) between the activation projection and judge-assigned trait scores (0–100).
  • Two experiments: (1) detection transfer, and (2) vector convergence, comparing the per-layer cosine alignment of implicitly- vs explicitly-extracted vectors.

Early findings

Detection generally transfers. When implicit prompts actually induce a trait, the explicit-extracted vectors detect it at r = 0.68–0.91, matching or exceeding explicit baselines. Hallucination reaches r = 0.85–0.91 (vs. 0.69–0.74 explicit); sycophancy's direct rewrite hits r = 0.77–0.83 (vs. 0.70–0.80).

"Evil" is the exception, for an elicitation reason, not a detection failure. Four situational rewrites (permission, pressure, direct address, personal motive) floor at mean trait scores ≤ 0.22/100; the trait barely shows up. Yet the same vector detects evil at r = 0.45–0.77 once a malicious disposition is described. So evil can be elicited implicitly, but only through described disposition, not situation alone.

It isn't an artifact. Mean-centered analysis confirms the transfer preserves the underlying projection–judge relationship rather than a mean-shift artifact.

Work in progress: I'm completing the vector-convergence analysis (per-layer cosine alignment) and extending beyond the first three traits. The full writeup and code have the details.
