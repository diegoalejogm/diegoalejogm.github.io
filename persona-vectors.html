Current research

Persona vectors for implicit elicitation

In progress

Can activation-level monitoring catch a model's traits when they're summoned indirectly? This is an active, early-stage project building on Anthropic's safety-research persona-vectors work.

  • LLMs
  • Interpretability
  • AI Safety
  • Evaluations

The question

Persona vectors are directions in a model's activation space that correspond to character traits such as sycophancy or "evil." Prior work shows they can be extracted by contrasting prompts that do and don't elicit a trait, and then used to monitor a trait at inference time or steer it during training.

That work mostly elicits traits explicitly: a system prompt that directly instructs the model to behave a certain way. But in the wild, traits are often induced implicitly, through roleplay, framing, or context rather than a direct instruction. My question:

Do persona vectors learned from explicit elicitation still reliably detect the same trait when it is elicited implicitly?

If they transfer, activation-level monitoring is more robust than it might look. If they don't, it points to a real gap in how we evaluate this kind of safety tooling.

Hypothesis

Persona vectors fit on explicit prompts will detect implicitly-elicited traits at meaningfully lower reliability than explicit ones, with the gap widening as the elicitation becomes more indirect.

Approach & setup

The experimental scaffold I'm building out:

  • Trait(s): starting with sycophancy, with room to add others.
  • Model: to be specified, an open-weights instruction-tuned LLM.
  • Vector extraction: contrast activations on explicit positive/negative system prompts to recover the trait direction.
  • Implicit elicitation set: prompts that induce the trait through roleplay and framing rather than direct instruction.
  • Metric: projection onto the persona vector vs. a behavioral judge, compared across explicit and implicit conditions.

Status & early findings

Results are in progress. This is a living document. I'll add the first findings and a figure here as the experiments produce them. The repository reflects the current state in the meantime.
