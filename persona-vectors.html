Persona vectors for implicit elicitation

Early results

Can activation-level monitoring catch a model's traits when they're summoned indirectly? Early results say mostly yes, with a revealing exception for "evil." Built on Anthropic's safety-research persona-vectors work.

TL;DR

Persona vectors trained on explicit prompts ("You are an evil assistant") are usually validated only on explicit elicitation. I tested whether they still detect traits elicited implicitly, through roleplay and situational framing, in Qwen2.5-7B. Detection transfers well for sycophancy and hallucination (r = 0.68–0.91, matching or beating explicit baselines). "Evil" is the exception, but for an elicitation reason: situational framing rarely produces evil at all, while a described malicious disposition both elicits it and is detected (r = 0.45–0.77).

The question

Persona vectors are directions in a model's activation space that correspond to character traits such as evil, sycophancy, or hallucination. They can be extracted by contrasting prompts that do and don't elicit a trait, and then used to monitor a trait at inference time or steer it during training.

Chen et al. (2025) validate persona-vector monitoring only on explicit system prompts like "You are an evil assistant." But in deployment, traits are usually induced implicitly, through roleplay and situational framing rather than a named instruction (e.g. "You are playing a villain in a dark fantasy novel who believes the ends always justify the means"). My question:

Do persona vectors learned from explicit elicitation still detect a trait when it is elicited implicitly?

Hypothesis

I expected detection to transfer when a trait is genuinely present, but to vary by elicitation route, and predicted that "evil" in particular might need to be stated as a disposition rather than implied by situation alone.

Approach

In brief: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; traits evil, sycophancy, and hallucination; three elicitation conditions (explicit, described, and contextual); detection measured as the correlation between the persona-vector projection and a judge's trait scores. The full writeup has the complete method and numbers.

What I found

Detection generally transfers. When an implicit prompt actually induces a trait, the explicit-extracted vector still detects it, matching or beating the explicit baseline for sycophancy and hallucination. And "implicit" splits in two: described prompts (naming the behavior, not the trait) transfer well, while contextual prompts (a situation only) depend on the trait.

"Evil" is the exception, for an elicitation reason, not a detection failure. Situational framing alone rarely produces evil at all; the same vector detects it fine once a malicious disposition is described. So evil can be elicited implicitly, but only through stated disposition, not situation alone.

Caveats: a single 7B model, small samples, and a correlational (projection-vs-judge) metric. Exact numbers, the results table, and the full limitations live in the writeup.
